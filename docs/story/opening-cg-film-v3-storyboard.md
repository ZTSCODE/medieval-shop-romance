# Opening CG Film v3: "The End of the Winter" (storyboard and narration, awaiting approval)

Status: 2026-10-03, proposal. Nothing is generated until the user approves this document. It replaces the v2.1 cut (`opening-cg-film.md`), which the user rejected on three counts: the narration was chopped into fragments; the film was about the hero, when it should be the Empire's story with his theft only hinted at the end; and it lacked the references' hard action, climax and stacked emotion.

## 1. What changes

- **The film is the Empire's story.** The hero never appears. The last movement shows only that something has been taken from the Empire and that the whole country is being shut and searched. What was taken is not shown or named. The game then opens on a man who has just escaped a hunt, and the player draws the line.
- **Narration in whole sentences**, in the manner of the prologue of *The Lord of the Rings*: eight sentences, about 150 words, with two long stretches where nobody speaks and the action carries.
- **Two action set pieces and a mobilisation**, built to the references' measured rhythm (§5): the winter falling on the towns, the breaking of the last free town, and the night every bell rings.
- **A face for what is lost:** the captain of the last free town, in a red cloak under a red banner. We see him laugh in summer, stand in the storm, fight in the gate and kneel in the snow. Red appears nowhere else in the film.
- **A sound thread:** a town bell rings for the harvest fair, rings in panic in the storm, hangs silent in the frost, falls burning from its tower in the sack; at the end every bell in the Empire rings at once.

## 3. New details this commits to (please confirm or strike)

1. **The Emperor's own fields have never frozen.** Behind a great wall the heartland still has harvests; that is where the grain comes from. The founder came "from the south" with grain for the same reason. This follows from the user's line that the *solution* to the winter could be stolen from the Empire: the Empire has always had it and kept it for itself.
2. **The towns that refused were taken by force.** The user's line says the founder swallowed the towns by means of the winter; this adds that the last hold-outs were stormed and burned as a lesson.
3. **The thing was kept in a locked chamber under the great granary.** We see only the open door and the empty place.
4. Numbers used as rhetoric, not canon: a hundred towns, a thousand wagons, the third year.
