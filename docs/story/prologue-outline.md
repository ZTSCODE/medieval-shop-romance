# Prologue Outline: "Out of the Wind" (proposal for review)

Scope (2026-10-07): this is the 2026-10-03 proposal and its implementation labels describe that date. Later user rulings and the current opening have superseded parts of it; proposed village, shop and tutorial extensions are not automatically approved or implemented. Use [the current canon](bible/README.md), [open questions](bible/open-questions.md) and [runtime history](../knowledge/opening-story-runtime.md) before continuing. The original proposal remains below for reference.

Status: proposal, 2026-10-03. English first. The whole prologue chapter from the first screen to the end of the tutorial. Parts 0–4 exist (0 and 1 in the game; 2–4 drafted in `opening-scenes-v2-draft.md`); parts 5–8 are new. Setting: `world-bible.md` §2a and §7.

**What the prologue must do:** set up the one conflict (the Empire rules through the White Winter; the hero carries a way to stand against it); bring him to the Lee; introduce the Messenger (mentor, first romance thread) and the woman with the lantern (the village's voice); teach the core loop (explore, talk, open the shop, trade, end the day); and end on a hook.

**Kept back for later chapters:** that he was a knight (only hinted); what the seed is and why the Empire burns villages for it; the Messenger's name; what the Lee will do when the Empire comes.

---

## 0. Chronicle (full screen, five pages) — *in the game*
The free towns; the White Winter that never ends; the man who followed the snow with grain wagons and spears and wrote each starving town onto his map; the Emperor, whose house the winter still serves. Now, at the ragged edge of the map, a man runs with forty riders behind him, because he has found a clue to standing against the winter.
*Then the player names the hero.*

## 1. The flight — *in the game (to be updated to the v2 draft)*
Night on the moor, black water to the knee. Behind him, horn calls he knows by heart; he taught half of them. A sack clutched to his chest that he has not let go of in three days. Ahead, a dark wood and a bank of mist.

## 2. The edge of the wood — *drafted*
A shape in the mist; he reaches for his knife before he has decided anything. It is a young courier with laurel in his hair. The courier warms the hero's frozen hands round a flask, notices the cloak worn inside out with imperial blue at the seam, and asks nothing. Dogs bay closer. The Messenger takes the hero's scarf to lead the hounds the wrong way: "You'd die for it, by the look of you, and I'd rather you didn't. Go."
*First card: Blackthorn Wood.*

## 3. The wood — *drafted* · teaches **exploring** and **talking**
The hero walks the wood alone. An hour later the Messenger catches him up, soaked to the knee, and gives back the scarf still warm from his neck. Two rules of the Lee: nobody asks where you came from; don't walk in alone, they've had soldiers before. He presses a bitter sloe into the hero's hand, points him up the badger track to the standing stones, and runs ahead.

## 4. The stones — *drafted* · teaches **exploring a second place**
The hero sits against the tallest stone with the sack in his lap and falls asleep for the first time in three days. He wakes at first light to the Messenger and half the village with pitchforks. A woman with a lantern looks at the inside-out cloak and the locked arms, and he waits for the question. "Bring him down, then. Feed him first." Nobody asks his name.

## 5. The Lee by morning — *new* · teaches **the village as a place**
He is fed in the woman's brewhouse (she is the Brewer; her name comes later). Through the door he sees the Lee properly: thirty roofs, thin people, and frost on the barley in what should be late summer. The winter has reached even here, and it is early. The Brewer is kind, but not soft: nobody stays for nothing. On the green stands the trader's shop, shuttered since the old trader died in spring. "Make it pay, or move on."
*New card: the Empty Shop.*

## 6. The first day of trade — *new* · teaches **opening the shop, buying, selling**
He opens the shutters on dust and an old set of scales. His first stock is what he carried in from the wood (sloes) and what the Messenger brings him to sell on. The first customer is the boy who held the dog at the stones, who wants something for his mother and has only one coin, worn smooth. The hero sells it to him for that coin anyway. Word goes round that the stranger trades fair.
*The day ends; the first night falls.*

## 7. The post — *new* · teaches **relationships** (the Messenger)
At dusk the Messenger comes back from his round outside the wood and sits on the shop step to share bread. Among the letters is a printed notice from the Empire: a man wanted alive, a description, a reward large enough to buy the Lee twice. The Messenger reads it, looks at the hero for a long moment, and feeds it to the lamp. "Didn't look a bit like you." He doesn't ask. The hero can choose how close to let him sit.

## 8. The seed — *new* · the hook
Alone that night, the hero opens the sack for the first time on screen. Old grain from the free towns, a fistful of it. Frost is creeping up the inside of the window. In the cold, one grain has split, and a green shoot has come up through the frost on his palm.
NARRATOR: Out on the moor, the snow is coming early. In your hand, something is growing.
*End of the prologue.*
