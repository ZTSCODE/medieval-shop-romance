# Opening Scenes v2 (English draft for review)

Status: draft, 2026-10-03. English only; Chinese and in-game wiring come after the user approves. Follows the chronicle "The Iron Peace" replacement in `opening-script-draft.md` v1.6 (free towns → White Winter → the founder's grain and spears → the Emperor → a man at the edge of the map with a clue to standing against the winter). Setting: `world-bible.md` §2a and §7.

**Who the hero is (kept back from the player, felt in every scene):** a knight in the Emperor's service, taken as a boy from a town the winter had broken. Sent to burn a hamlet for hiding seed corn, he found what it was hiding: old grain from the free towns, putting up green shoots through the frost. He took the sack and rode. The forty riders hunting him are his own company.

**What the player learns in these scenes, and how:** that he knows his pursuers' horns and drills (he was one of them); that he will not let go of a sack (it matters more than his life); that his cloak is imperial blue worn inside out (the Messenger notices). Nobody says the word *knight*, and nobody says what is in the sack.

**Register:** epic Western fantasy, in the line of Absolum, Hades and *The Lord of the Rings*. Plain words, concrete detail, room for silence. Dialogue sounds like people, not like a chronicle.

Format: `NARRATOR`, `YOU` (the player's chosen name), `MESSENGER`, other speakers by role. `▸` marks a choice; italics are the prompt.

## Scene 1. The flight (narration, after the chronicle and naming)

NARRATOR: Your lungs burn. The ground goes soft underfoot, then softer, then gives way to black water that takes you to the knee.

NARRATOR: Behind you, horns call to one another across the moor. You know every one of those calls. You taught half of them.

NARRATOR: The sack is still clutched against your chest. You have not let go of it in three days. You will not let go of it now.

NARRATOR: Ahead, the land rises into trees, black against a grey sky, and from between them a bank of white mist rolls out to meet you.

## Scene 2. At the edge of the wood (`story.messenger.edge`, starts by itself)

NARRATOR: Something moves in the mist. You stop. So does it.

NARRATOR: A shape, man-high. A glint of brass at its belt.

*What do you do?*

▸ **[Draw your knife.]**
　NARRATOR: Your hand finds the hilt before you have decided anything. Eleven years of drill, and it is still the first thing your hand does.
　MESSENGER: Put that away. I bruise easily.

▸ **[Hold still.]**
　NARRATOR: You make yourself very small. It doesn't help.
　MESSENGER: I can see you, you know.

▸ **"Who's there?"**
　MESSENGER: Nobody worth a knife.

NARRATOR: A young man steps out of the mist, breathing hard. Laurel in his hair, a satchel of letters at his hip, a towel slung round his neck as if he has been running for hours.

MESSENGER: Oh. You're not a deer.

YOU: Who sent you?

MESSENGER: The post sent me. Nobody sends me after people. I'm not fast enough to be frightening.

NARRATOR: He looks past you, down the slope, to where lanterns bob across the moor. His smile thins.

MESSENGER: Someone sent them, though.

YOU: They won't come into the trees in the dark.

MESSENGER: No. But they'll come at first light, and they've got dogs.

NARRATOR: You don't turn round. You don't trust yourself to.

MESSENGER: You've been running.

YOU: So have you.

MESSENGER: I'm paid to. You look like stopping would cost you.

NARRATOR: He holds out a flask worked with a pair of brass wings.

MESSENGER: Drink. It's still warm.

NARRATOR: You take it one-handed. The other arm stays wrapped round the sack. Your fingers are so cold the brass almost burns.

NARRATOR: He notices. Before you can pull away, his hand closes over yours on the flask.

MESSENGER: Saints. You're freezing.

NARRATOR: His hand is hot from the road. He holds on until the flask stops shaking.

NARRATOR: Then his eyes go to the sack, and from the sack to your cloak: grey, mud-stained, worn inside out. Where the seam has split, a thread of imperial blue shows through.

MESSENGER: Nice cloak. Wrong way out.

*What do you say?*

▸ **"It's warmer this way."**
　MESSENGER: Is it.
　NARRATOR: He doesn't believe you. He doesn't press, either.

▸ **"Are you going to tell them?"**
　MESSENGER: Tell who what? I carry letters. I don't read them.

▸ **[Say nothing.]**
　NARRATOR: He looks at your face a moment longer, as if he were trying to place it. Then he lets it go.

NARRATOR: Down on the moor a dog gives tongue, and another answers it. Closer than before.

MESSENGER: Right. Here's what we'll do.

MESSENGER: There's a stream past those trees. Walk in it, not beside it, and keep going until the wood thins.

YOU: And you?

MESSENGER: Give me something you've been wearing.

NARRATOR: You hand him your scarf. He winds it round his own neck without a word, over the towel.

MESSENGER: I'll take the dogs a mile the other way, then catch you up. Don't drop that sack in the water.

YOU: You don't know what's in it.

MESSENGER: No. But you'd die for it, by the look of you, and I'd rather you didn't. Go.

> Cards appear: **You**, **Blackthorn Wood**. Hint: drag yourself onto Blackthorn Wood. Walking it brings **the Messenger** and **Sloes**.

## Scene 3. In the wood (`story.messenger.thorn_rules`, drag You onto the Messenger)

NARRATOR: An hour later he catches you up, soaked to the knee and pleased with himself.

MESSENGER: They went the wrong way. Dogs love a scarf.

NARRATOR: He gives you back the scarf. It is still warm from his neck.

MESSENGER: So. Two things, since nobody's told you.

MESSENGER: One. In the Lee, nobody asks where you came from. Don't ask them either.

MESSENGER: Two. Don't walk into the village alone. They've had soldiers before, and they've learned to meet strangers at the door with a pitchfork. Wait at the standing stones on the track, and I'll bring you in.

NARRATOR: He crouches by a blackthorn, picks a sloe, frowns at it, and drops it into your palm.

MESSENGER: Keep those. Bitter now. After the first frost they're worth something.

YOU: What is the Lee?

MESSENGER: The last village before the map runs out. People stopped running there when the winter took their towns. It's too far out for the Empire to bother with. Which, by the look of you, is the right place.

MESSENGER: North of here there's a badger track. Follow it to the stones.

YOU: You're not coming?

MESSENGER: I'm faster. I'll run ahead and tell them someone's coming. They'll feed you before they ask you anything. That's the custom.

NARRATOR: He's already walking backwards, grinning.

MESSENGER: Don't look so worried. I'll be there when you arrive. I always am.

NARRATOR: Then he turns and runs into the trees.

NARRATOR: You wind the scarf back round your neck. It still holds the warmth of him.

> The Messenger leaves; **Badger Track** appears. Hint: follow the track. Walking it brings **The Stones**.

## Scene 4. The stones (new; plays when The Stones appear)

NARRATOR: The stones stand in a ring at the top of the track: grey, leaning, older than anything the Empire ever built. Below them, in a fold of the hills out of the wind, lie a handful of roofs and a single thread of smoke.

NARRATOR: You sit down with your back to the tallest stone and the sack in your lap. For the first time in three days, nobody is behind you.

NARRATOR: You only mean to close your eyes for a moment.

NARRATOR: When you open them, the Messenger is crouched in front of you, and behind him, in the grey of first light, stands what looks like half the village. Pitchforks. A boy with a dog on a rope. A woman holding a lantern up to see your face.

MESSENGER: Told you I'd be here.

WOMAN WITH THE LANTERN: Is he hurt?

MESSENGER: Cold. Hungry. Rude.

NARRATOR: She looks at you for a long moment: at the cloak worn inside out, at the arms still locked round the sack. You wait for the question.

WOMAN WITH THE LANTERN: Bring him down, then. Feed him first.

NARRATOR: Nobody asks your name. Nobody asks what is in the sack. Someone puts a blanket round your shoulders, someone else takes your arm, and the Lee takes you in out of the wind.

> End of the opening. Next: the Lee by day, the empty shop, and the first time the player trades.

## Notes for review

- **New in this draft:** the hero's past is felt, never stated: he knows the riders' horn calls and taught half of them; eleven years of drill put his hand on his knife; his imperial-blue cloak is worn inside out; he will not let go of the sack. The Messenger's earlier "Have we met?" moves into the third choice, where he tries to place the hero's face.
- **Scene 4 is new.** It ends the opening with the village's custom (feed him first, ask nothing) and needs one small mechanic: a scene that plays by itself when The Stones appear. The woman with the lantern is a villager to be named later (Gaelic name, plain trade epithet), likely the person who will lend him the empty shop.
- **Removed from the game version:** "You're real" and the mist that opens only for him; the old magical rules of the wood. The wood is ordinary; the danger is the riders at first light.
- **Card text to update after approval:** You: *On the run.* Blackthorn Wood: *Wet, thorny, and dark enough to hide in.* The Stones: *Wait here for him.* The sack could become a card of its own later (the seed), but not in the opening.
