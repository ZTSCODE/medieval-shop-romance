## 1. What the references do (observed, shot by shot)

Both were downloaded and cut into per-shot contact sheets (`outputs/opening-cg-film/refs/trailers/`).

- **Absolum reveal trailer** (Dotemu, 110 s; the animated part is 0:10–1:02). No history lesson. It opens on one emblem of power (the tower), cuts to frightened ordinary faces in a cellar, shows one concrete act of oppression (masked soldiers seize a hooded man, the longest shot at 6 s), then the answer arrives and the cutting drops below one second with white flash frames. Each section has its own two-colour palette with one accent.
- **Hades II launch trailer "Limits"** (90 s). Opens in the middle of a confrontation. The voice is a character speaking *to the hero*, and it lays out the stakes as a choice; the pictures show what the voice describes. Extreme close-ups (eyes, mouth, hands, feet), a silhouette against a huge moon, a long side-on silhouette run, ink-wipe and white-flash transitions. Speech beats hold 2–3 s; action beats 0.1–1.5 s. It returns to the opening confrontation, lands the hero's one-line answer, and smashes to black.

**The method we borrow:** start in the middle of the action; show the world through concrete incidents and reaction shots, never a list of dates; one palette per section with a single accent colour; a slow section, then silence, then the fastest section; end on one line and one image.

## 2. What changes from the chronicle

| Chronicle v1.6 | Film v2.0 |
|---|---|
| Five pages in date order, narrator explains | Starts on the flight; history arrives as a hard cut into the past and is told in six short spoken lines |
| "He has found a clue…" stated in words | The clue is **shown**: grain sprouting through frost in a cellar, and a man who lets his torch go out instead of burning it |
| Ends on a sentence | Ends on one image: a green blade at the mouth of the sack |
| One still per page | 33 shots cut into 45 pieces of 0.5–4.8 s; 69.9 s in all |

The world line is unchanged (user ruling 2026-10-03): the founder used the White Winter to take the free towns one by one; the winter still serves the Empire; the hero has fled to the edge of the Empire's reach because he found a clue to standing against the winter.

**Two things this film commits to that were still proposals** (world-bible §7), and the only shots they touch:
1. *The clue is old seed grain that sprouts in frost.* Shots 19, 21, 33.
2. *He was one of the men sent to burn it.* Shots 18, 21, 22 (a gloved hand with a torch, soldiers waiting above). Nothing says "knight"; his face on the run and his coat are the ones in the game.

## 3. Spoken text (one voice, about 45 words)

An old voice from the edge of the map, unhurried. It never explains the seed.

1. There were towns here once. Free towns.
2. Then the winter came. And it did not leave.
3. One man followed the snow. He came with bread. And with spears.
4. Gate by gate, the towns went onto his map.
5. They call it an Empire now.
6. And it keeps one law: where the Emperor's grain does not go… nothing grows.
7. *(silence through the cellar)*
8. Forty riders.
9. One man.
10. And a handful of seed.

The cut after line 6 lands the word *grows* on the first green thing in the film.

## 6. Sound

- One music track, used twice: its opening pulses from the horn through 17 (cut dead on the torch, with a rung-out tail), then its last 32 beats under the hunt, so that its final hit lands on the rearing horse and its own tail carries the last shot. Cuts in II and IV sit on its beats (125 bpm).
- Each generated clip carries its own effects (water, wind, horn, hooves, fire); they are cross-faded under the music.
- The voice is one recording, not generated per clip.

## 7. Production

MiniMax H3 run locally (FL2VA model, first frame or first and last frame), 1696×960, 24 fps, one generation per shot with handles, 8-step turbo for this verification pass. Keyframes are painted separately per shot against one style frame and the hero's game portrait. The narration is also generated by H3 and checked word for word. Temporary music: "Juggernaut" by Scott Buckley (CC BY 4.0). Parameters, takes and scripts: `outputs/opening-cg-film/`; notes: `docs/knowledge/opening-cg-film-production.md`.

## 8. Known weak points in this cut (for the review)

- Shot 12: the founder waits on foot outside the gate, but is mounted in shot 11.
- Shots 31 and 32: late in each take the mist turns into rounded cloud puffs; the cut leaves before that.
- Shot 04: the distant wood is drawn as conifers; the wood in shots 31–33a is blackthorn.
- Nobody has listened to the mix: levels, sync points and words were verified by measurement only (integrated loudness about -16.6 LUFS, peak -2.2 dBFS).
